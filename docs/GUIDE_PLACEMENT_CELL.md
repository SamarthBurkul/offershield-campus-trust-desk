# OfferShield guide for the placement cell

OfferShield is your desk for checking suspicious job and internship offers that students receive. Students send a request with the message and screenshots; you check it, the TPO head records the outcome, and confirmed scams are added to your college's registry so the next student who looks up the same UPI ID or website sees a warning straight away.

OfferShield never decides for you. Its warning signs (fee requested, look-alike website, a contact already confirmed as fraud) only help you look in the right place.

## Your daily routine (10–20 minutes)

1. **Open the Case desk.** Start with *Unassigned* and *Stale* (no activity for a few days). High priority means the student was asked for money or has already paid: handle those first.
2. **Take a case:** assign it to yourself. The student sees that it is under review.
3. **Check it.** Look at the message, the links and contacts, the evidence and the warning signs. Typical checks:
   - Is the sender's email or website the company's official domain? (Compare with the *Registry → Recruiters* list or the company's official careers page.)
   - Was money asked for? Genuine employers never charge to apply, interview, train or join.
   - Call the company's officially published HR number if needed — never a number from the message.
   - See *Related cases*: the same UPI ID or a near-identical message from other students.
4. **Talk to the student when needed.** *Request information* asks a question and pauses the case until they reply; *Message* just informs them. Students see these. **Internal notes are never shown to students** — put your working notes there.
5. **Recommend an outcome** (officers) with a short reason. The TPO head is notified.
6. **Students page:** in colleges that approve students manually, approve new registrations after checking the name against your enrolment list.

## Recommendations vs decisions

| | Who | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Recommendation** | officers and the TPO head | Your suggested outcome and why. Internal only; the student does not see it. |
| **Decision** | **TPO head only** | The final outcome. Closes the case, records the checks made, and sends the student your message. |

Outcomes: **Confirmed fraud** (you select which UPI IDs, websites or numbers are confirmed — these go into the registry), **Verified genuine**, **Inconclusive**, or **Dismissed** (for example a duplicate of another case). The basis and checks stay internal; the student sees only your message.

If new facts appear, the TPO head can **reopen** a closed case with a reason. The old decision is kept in the history, and anything it added to the fraud registry is withdrawn unless another confirmed case supports it.

## Advisories

When a scam is going around, warn the students at risk:

1. From a confirmed-fraud case, choose *Draft advisory from this case* (or create one under *Advisories*).
2. Pick the years and branches to warn; the audience count shows how many students will be notified.
3. Officers save a draft; **only the TPO head publishes**. Published advisories cannot be edited — if something is wrong, withdraw it and publish a corrected one.
4. The advisory page shows how many students have confirmed "I've read this".

## What never to publish (or write to students)

- **No student names, emails, roll numbers or photos** — not in advisories, not in messages to other students. OfferShield blocks the reporting student's name and email in advisories, but you must avoid any other detail that identifies them.
- **No Aadhaar, PAN, bank or card numbers, and no OTPs** — never ask a student for them.
- **Only confirmed findings.** A student's report is an allegation until the TPO head records a confirmed-fraud decision. Do not call a company a fraud in an advisory without that.
- **Never say an offer is "safe" or "guaranteed".** Even a verified recruiter can be impersonated. Say what you checked: "This invitation matches what the company publishes officially."
- Do not publish internal notes, the decision basis, or other colleges' information.

## Accounts and passwords

- A student or officer who forgot their password asks you in person or by phone: *Students → Reset password* (officers and the TPO head) or *Staff → Reset password* (TPO head, for officers). Give the temporary password directly to that person; it is shown only once and must be changed at the next sign-in.
- If an account may be misused, **Disable** it — the person is signed out immediately.

## Good practice

- Reply within a working day, especially when money was requested — students are often given a 24-hour deadline by scammers.
- Keep notes factual: what you checked, when, and the result.
- Review the *Stats* page weekly: open and stale cases, fee demands, and how many students read each advisory.
